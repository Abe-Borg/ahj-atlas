Take the guided tour

The guided tour walks every part of the window on a practice project: Sampleton, a fictional hyperscale data center bundled with the app. A card beside each control says what it does, and you can click the real controls as you go. The practice project is built in memory when the tour starts and discarded when it ends. It is never in your projects, your spending or your exports, nothing in it is sent to Claude, and nothing is spent. Your own projects are set aside untouched and come back as you left them.

Start the guided tour

Or start at a chapter:

  1. Your practice workspace: the sidebar, your API key and spending, help and updates.
  2. Start a project: address, country, discipline, project context, and research now or later.
  3. Read the report: the header, the stages, evidence statuses, questions to resolve, notes and tabs.
  4. Contacts, codes and sources: contacts, NFPA screening, editions, your documents, the source register and spending.
  5. Keep the research moving: continue or extend, stop, export and delete.
  6. Chat with Atlas: the panel, a reply and its evidence, proposal cards and the message box.
  7. Finish.

Continue moves on and Back goes back. End, or Escape and then End the tour, returns your workspace at any step; drag a card by its title if it covers something. While the tour runs, your own projects are held in the sidebar, Start research is held, and the practice project's conversation is a recording: its message box and Ask Atlas are held. Every change to the practice project, such as saving an answer, renaming it, applying a card or continuing research, is refused with a message. Exports work, so you can download the practice project as a PDF or Excel file. A reload picks the tour up at the same step.

Guided tour in the top bar starts it again at any time; it pulses until you have finished the tour once. In the desktop app, Help → Take the guided tour starts it too.

Start a project

  1. Open API & spending and enter your Anthropic API key. The connection checks the key; each request checks access to the model it will use. The sidebar shows Claude connected only after Anthropic accepts the key, and Claude disconnected when no key is entered or the key is rejected. Web search must be enabled in your Anthropic Console.
  2. Enter the complete project address and check Country beside it. AHJ Atlas researches projects in the United States and Canada; the country follows the address until you choose one, and the form warns if your choice contradicts a postal code, a state with its ZIP code, or a country named in the address. For a Canadian project, research looks for provincial or territorial code adoption, municipal by-laws, provincial safety authorities and CSA and ULC standards, and reads French-language sources in Quebec. Choose your discipline, or select Other — enter your discipline and type your specialty. Add the scope and permit date when known. Building use starts as Hyperscale data center and stays visible beside the required fields. To change it, choose Other — enter building use under Add project context and type your own. The new-project form stays in this page until you start research or confirm clearing it. For a site without an assigned street address, add a parcel number (such as an APN, PID, roll or lot number, or legal land description) or a site description under Add project context.
  3. Choose Research now or Research later, and start.
  4. Inspect findings, evidence passages, and questions to resolve. Download a PDF, Excel workbook, Excel essentials, or JSON research record.

Rename a project

Open the project and edit Project name, then choose Save name. You can rename a project after research has started, including while a stage is running and after the report is finished. The new name is saved on the project and shown in the sidebar, project header, chat, Activity, and PDF, Excel, and JSON exports. Renaming does not stop research, restart a stage, or change saved findings.

Export project addresses

Choose Export addresses in the sidebar to download the addresses of several projects in one file. Every project starts checked. Clear the ones you do not want, or choose Clear all and check the ones you do. Type in the filter to narrow a long list by name, address, discipline or country; Select shown and Clear shown then change only the projects you can see, and projects hidden by the filter keep their checks. Choose CSV or Excel workbook and Export.

The file has one row per project, in the order of the sidebar: project name, address, country, parcel or site description, and discipline. It does not include report findings, contacts or costs. CSV opens in Excel and imports into mapping and mail-merge tools. In the desktop app you choose where to save it, as with other exports.

Real-time and batch

Research now begins immediately. The app updates as each research stage and source reading finishes.

Research later uses Anthropic’s Message Batches API. Model token prices are 50% lower; search fees are unchanged. Research has dependent stages, so it requires several batch submissions. Each batch has a 24-hour processing window and can expire. A complete report can take longer than 24 hours.

Keep the local application running to advance stages automatically. Closing the browser is fine. If the computer sleeps or the backend stops, batches already submitted continue at Anthropic. Reopen the app and reconnect your key to retrieve results and continue.

Fire protection and NFPA standards

Fire protection research screens individual NFPA standards for your project, including system-specific candidates based on building use and notes. Each finding distinguishes applicability from evidence status and gives the adopted edition and source when established. A standard on the checklist is not automatically a legal requirement. Unknown editions remain unconfirmed.

For an older report, open Codes & standards → Research NFPA standards. This starts paid, focused research and a new report. If you have saved answers that research has not used yet, choose Research with saved answers first: an answer can change the jurisdiction and contacts that the focused update keeps. It retains completed jurisdiction/contact work, saved sources and spending, and starts a new research round with the full search and read allowance. Choose real-time or batch in the dialog. Your previous report remains available while research runs.

PDF reports include a separate NFPA section, and Excel exports include an NFPA standards worksheet. Add system details such as battery chemistry, suppression type and fuel storage through the normal project-context update when known.

Other disciplines: referenced standards

Architecture, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and civil projects are screened the same way against a checklist of referenced standards for that discipline, such as ASHRAE 62.1 and NFPA 90A for mechanical or ASCE 7 and ACI 318 for structural. Canadian projects use Canadian lists, such as CSA B52 and CSA B149.1 for mechanical. Building use, scope and notes add candidates: a data center adds ASHRAE 90.4 for mechanical, for example. A custom discipline uses the checklists its name matches (MEP combines mechanical, electrical and plumbing); a discipline that matches none keeps the original research. Each finding gives applicability, evidence status and the adopted edition and source when established; a standard on the checklist is not automatically a legal requirement.

Open Codes & standards → Research referenced standards for the same paid, focused update the NFPA checklist has. PDF reports include a referenced standards section, and Excel exports include a Referenced standards worksheet.

Project chat

A project name is required when creating research. Within a saved project, choose Chat with Atlas in the project header, or the launcher at the bottom right of the window, to ask about the report, sources, research notes and saved question answers. The chat is a floating panel beside the report: drag it by its header, resize it from its corner, expand it, or minimize it to the launcher. It stays open while you read the other tabs and switch projects, and the app remembers where you left it. Enter sends a message; Shift+Enter starts a new line; Escape minimizes the panel. Choose a Reply depth for each message: Standard (Sonnet 5.5, high reasoning) is the default for most questions, and Premium (Opus 5.5, high reasoning) is for the hardest analysis and costs about twice as much per token. Each project has its own conversation and draft. Switching projects keeps pending replies with their original project.

Each message starts with saved report and research previews, a source register, retrieved-source excerpts and earlier conversation. The assistant can look up anything else saved for that project and cite sources you can open. It may add general professional knowledge, labeled separately from project evidence. Chat never changes the project on its own.

Attaching documents. Choose Attach document under the message box, or drop files on it, to send up to five PDF, HTML or plain-text files (50 MB each) with a message, such as a Fire Marshal's letter or owner criteria. Each file is added to the project's Sources as your document as soon as you choose it, free and before anything is sent, so it stays there even if you do not send the message. The list above the message box shows what goes with the message; × removes a document from the message but keeps it in Sources. The reply is told which documents you attached and can read and cite them. The sent message lists its documents; choose one to open it in Sources.

Attaching images. Choose Attach image, paste a screenshot into the message box, or drop image files on it, to send up to five PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP pictures (20 MB each) with a message: a photo of a plan review comment, a drawing detail, a screenshot. The app resizes each image on this computer to the size the model would see anyway and removes its metadata (a photo's location, camera and date tags) before it is sent with your message. An image is not a source: it is not added to Sources or exports, Claude is told that text it reads from an image is information you provided and cannot be cited, and × on an unsent image deletes it. The sent message shows each image; choose one to see it full size.

Actions you approve. Chat can propose five kinds of change: answering a saved question, dismissing one, saving a finding as a note in the report, starting a research round (with new project context, or the focused standards update: NFPA standards on a fire protection project, referenced standards for another discipline), and correcting the project address, country or parcel/site description. Each proposal appears below the reply as a card with an Apply button, and nothing changes until you choose it. Apply saves exactly what the card shows, through the same steps as a question card or the Continue research dialog. Answer, dismiss and note cards are free; answers and dismissals can be reopened on the question card. Research and location cards start a paid research round; choose Research now or Research later (batch) on the card. They wait while research, a chat reply or an outstanding request is running, and they can no longer be applied once research has run after they were proposed; ask chat again if the change still applies. An answer chat found in a source names that source, because an applied answer is kept as information you provided. Activity records each applied proposal.

The assistant works project first, then the web: it answers from the saved records when they settle a question, and searches the web and reads public pages on its own when they don't, for example for a code section's wording, an agency's current fee or form, a product listing, a finding that is still unconfirmed, or anything you ask it to check or bring up to date. Under each finished reply, Checked the web counts its searches and page reads, or From project records says it used none. Pages it reads are added to the project's source register, so later chat, research and the report can use them, and you can open the supporting passage for each cited claim. Search results appear as [Web] Search lead: a snippet to follow up, not verified evidence. The assistant reads only pages it has found in searches, saved sources, links on pages it read, or addresses you include in your message. When the app's own reader cannot open a page (for example, a site that blocks it), the assistant can retrieve it through Anthropic's web fetch instead; those pages join the source register too. Web searches are localized to the project's city and state and leave out AI-assistant answers, forums and how-to content sites. Each reply can use up to 20 web searches and 30 page reads (web fetches included), separate from research's allowance.

Report notes. When chat settles a point worth keeping, it can propose a note, or you can ask it to save one. Applying the card is free and adds the note to a Notes section on Overview and to the PDF, Excel and JSON exports. Notes stay when research rebuilds the report. They are labeled as saved from chat and are not re-verified by research. Choose Remove on a note to delete it.

While a reply runs, its text appears as it is written, with the current step, such as the search being run or the source being read. Answers can include headings, lists, tables and code. Copy answer under a reply copies its text; Copy table above a table copies it so it pastes into Excel as cells or into Word as a table. When Anthropic's safeguards decline a message, the API re-runs it on the model Anthropic recommends for that kind of decline, where one exists, and the reply says which model answered; its cost is estimated at each model's rates. If the message is still declined, the reply shows Declined by Claude with the reason and a button to try the same message on the suggested model, which uses different safeguards.

Each message uses real-time API pricing, and each reply shows its estimated cost. There is no spending limit. A reply can use up to 20 requests, 60 lookups and about 20 minutes of lookups, then writes its final answer from what it found. Web searches cost $10 per 1,000 plus the tokens of their results. Project evidence is cached for an hour, so follow-up questions within the hour cost less. A change of model can require another cache write. Web search fees are the same in every reply depth. A web-tool charge may be conservatively estimated when the provider omits per-prompt usage; Activity identifies these estimates. Chat runs alongside research and does not wait for research to finish. Stop reply prevents more work after the current request finishes and its cost is recorded. Conversations are saved locally and sent with relevant project context to Anthropic when you send a message. Restarting the app does not automatically resend interrupted messages.

Adding your own documents

Open the Sources tab and use Add a document you have to add a PDF, HTML or plain-text file up to 50 MB, such as a Fire Marshal's letter, an amendment an AHJ emailed you, or owner or insurer criteria. The app reads its text on this computer and saves it as a source marked your document. Chat can quote it in the next reply; choose Continue research to include it in the research and a new report. Claude treats it as evidence you provided, not as a page retrieved from the issuing agency. Save Word files as PDF first. A scanned PDF needs text recognition (OCR) before it can be added. Adding the same file again updates the same source. You can also choose Attach document in Chat with Atlas to add a document and ask about it in the same message.

Answering questions

Each Questions to resolve card lets you enter what you know and choose Save answer, Dismiss a question that is not a priority, or Ask Atlas. Ask Atlas sends that question through Chat with Atlas, using the reply depth selected there. The reply appears in the conversation and can propose an answer; nothing is saved until you choose Apply. If it proposes a report note or an answer to another question instead, applying that card leaves the question you asked about open, so a saved finding never resolves a question by itself. Answer or dismiss the question on its card when it is settled. The open count updates immediately. Expand Answered & dismissed to edit an answer or reopen a question. Responses are saved with the project and included in exports.

Saving does not start research or use Claude credits. Choose Research with saved answers to start another round with your answers and dismissals. Newly saved information reopens research so its implications can be checked. Answers remain user-provided context; dismissing a question does not establish compliance or non-applicability. All fields under Add project context, including Additional context, are optional.

Understanding evidence

Source-supported: the model identified support and the app matched a quoted passage to retrieved source text. A code row's quoted passage must also contain its edition year, and a standard's adoption passage must say it, or the code that references it, is adopted or in force, so a publisher's catalog listing is not enough. A listed email address or phone number must appear in a fully read source the finding cites, not only in a search snippet; otherwise it is removed and the finding is not source-supported. This is not an independent certification of legal applicability or design compliance.

Conditional: the finding depends on an assumption or unresolved jurisdiction. Conflicting: sources disagree. Unconfirmed: the evidence or exact edition has not been established.

Search discoveries are listed separately from sources read as evidence. A source read as evidence shows when its text was retrieved; finding the same page again in a later search does not change that date, so older text never looks freshly checked. A lead shows when it was discovered. PDF and Excel exports show each finding's evidence status and mark any quotation that was not found in the retrieved source with Supporting passage needs confirmation. Scanned PDF pages can be inspected visually, but a visually transcribed quotation remains unconfirmed until readable evidence supports it. Current public contacts, effective dates, transition rules, and adopted editions should be confirmed with the responsible authority when unclear.

Costs and limits

The app uses Sonnet 5.5 for research and Opus 5.5 for an evidence check and the final report. Contacts use medium effort; jurisdiction and code research use high effort; both Opus stages use high effort. The evidence check can investigate missing or conflicting sources before the report is written. Model rates are based on Anthropic’s documentation checked October 7, 2026.

The app does not limit spending. Each project shows its estimated cost to date, the part that came from chat, and an estimate for requests still pending. API & spending shows estimated spending today and across all projects. Estimates use recorded token usage at list prices; native search has variable internal token usage, and the provider invoice is authoritative. To set a hard cap, use your workspace spend limit in Anthropic Console; the app reports when Anthropic stops a request at that limit.

Each research round is bounded to 80 searches and 100 source reads (including pages fetched through Anthropic's web fetch); research is also bounded to 12 requests per stage and at most two active local research tasks. A new round with the full allowance starts when you continue with new context or saved answers, correct the location, or run a focused NFPA or standards update; continuing without changes keeps what remains, and the dialog shows it before paid work starts. Each research request can read up to 300,000 tokens of input; a long investigation restarts from its saved checkpoint at 200,000. The final report's reviewer receives up to 600,000 characters of source excerpts and up to 80,000 characters of each research stage's brief. Research responses allow up to 60,000 output tokens; the final report allows 100,000, including thinking. Project chat sets no token limit of its own: each chat request can write up to 128,000 output tokens, the model’s maximum, and read as much input as the model’s context window holds. These are ceilings, not targets or fixed charges. The Opus evidence check has its own limit of 6 requests, 15 searches and 20 source reads within the round's totals. Real-time work has an active research time limit of 45 minutes; a response already in flight can finish afterward, with a 30-minute response deadline. Batch waiting time has separate provider deadlines.

If research pauses with unfinished stages, choose Continue research or Finish with saved evidence. A partial report still requires a paid final review. Research stages keep their selected mode unless you explicitly change it on continuation. Chat always uses real-time pricing, including on a batch project.

Stopping, resuming, and uncertain requests

Stop research prevents new requests and asks Anthropic to cancel pending batches. In-flight work can still complete and be billed. The app keeps polling known batches to reconcile results.

If a real-time stream fails after Anthropic starts generating, the app records an estimated cost from partial usage and streamed output (or the pending reservation) and marks it estimated in Activity. Research retries after 30 and 60 seconds within its request and active-time limits; a third recorded error pauses the stage. Chat ends the reply as Failed with a note to send a new message. If the connection ends before generation starts, the request's estimated cost stays pending for manual reconciliation. For a batch, find its ID in your Anthropic Console and use Reconcile original batch after it finishes. The app matches the exact request identifier before importing. After the 29-day batch-result retention window, or if results cannot be accessed, the app stops automatic retrieval and keeps the estimate pending for manual reconciliation.

When automatic reconciliation is unavailable, open Activity → Resolve uncertain charge. Confirm in the Anthropic Console that the original request finished, failed, or was canceled, and record its charge. Do not release a reservation for work whose outcome is still unknown.

Adding project context or a follow-up question when resuming reopens the research stages so the findings can be checked. When all research stages are already complete, continuing without new context runs only a paid final report using the saved sources. Older saved reports are preserved; explicitly continuing an older project adds the new Opus evidence check when needed. Recorded costs are retained.

Correcting the project address

You can correct a project's address without deleting the project, for example to fix a typo or a ZIP or postal code, or when a new-tract site receives its assigned street address. Choose Continue research (or Review or extend report) and edit Project address, Country or Parcel number or site description in the dialog. The address needs 8–500 characters, the same as a new project. An address in the other country needs the country corrected with it.

A changed address, country or site description reopens jurisdiction research and the stages that depend on it: contacts, codes, the Opus evidence check and the final report. Activity records the old and new values. The address lookup, evidence excerpts, project chat and PDF, Excel and JSON exports use the corrected address. Exports also list the site description and earlier addresses. Saved sources and recorded costs are kept; the correction starts a new research round with the full search and read allowance. Resubmitting the same address does not reopen research.

Wait for running research, an active chat reply or an outstanding request to finish, and resolve any uncertain charge, before correcting the address. An address correction cannot be combined with Finish with saved evidence or a focused standards update. Project chat can also propose a correction as a card; applying it follows the same rules, and removing a site description still takes the dialog.

The desktop menu bar

The desktop app's menu bar holds AHJ Atlas's own commands. Each one does exactly what the matching button in the window does.

  • File: New research (Ctrl+N), Export addresses…, API and spending… (Ctrl+,), and Exit, which closes the window the same way its close button does, asking first if research or a chat reply is still running.
  • View: the open project's Overview, Contacts, Codes and standards, Sources and Activity (Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+5); Chat with Atlas (Ctrl+J); Zoom in, Zoom out and Actual size (Ctrl+Plus, Ctrl+Minus, Ctrl+0); full screen (F11); and Reload page (Ctrl+R), which reloads the window if it ever stops responding. Unsent drafts can be lost on a reload.
  • Help: Take the guided tour, User guide (F1), Why trust it?, Diagnostics…, Check for updates… in the installed app, and About AHJ Atlas.

Cut, copy, paste, undo and select all keep their usual keys in every text field.

Diagnostics and troubleshooting

Open API & spending → Diagnostics to inspect saved progress, request history, timing, usage, and errors. Choose a project or the entire workspace, then select Download diagnostics to save a troubleshooting file.

Diagnostics are local and persist after restarting. The latest 5,000 detailed events are retained. Older projects still show saved request summaries, but detailed tracing starts with version 1.2.0. Viewing or downloading diagnostics does not spend Claude credits.

Diagnostics also show whether the computer or Anthropic held research back. While the app runs it samples its own event-loop delay, CPU and memory, and the computer’s CPU and free memory, every 5 seconds; Starved / throttled counts host starvation episodes and Anthropic rate-limit or overload responses, and Resources and starvation lists them beside the app’s own spent allowances. Samples hold numbers only.

Keys, prompts, source text, and model thinking are excluded. Review error details before sharing the file; nothing is uploaded automatically.

Your data and API key

Research is stored in the app’s data folder. If you put the app in a synced folder, that folder’s normal sync settings apply. The local server accepts connections only from this computer.

Your key is held in backend memory by default. Optional Windows storage protects it with your Windows account and saves the encrypted credential in Local AppData, outside the app’s project folder. The key field is not intentionally copied into browser storage, reports, or the research database. Secrets typed into project notes or chat are not excluded by that separation.

Project details and relevant source content are sent to Anthropic to perform research. The address can also be sent to the US Census geocoder when applicable. Public research URLs are retrieved from their original sites. Batch requests are stored by Anthropic during processing; consult its current data-retention documentation for details.

The isolated research browser uses a fresh profile without your browser sign-ins. It reads public pages and does not log into services, submit applications, send emails, or purchase documents.

Launch and stop

Double-click Start AHJ Atlas.cmd to open the application. Double-click Stop AHJ Atlas.cmd to finish in-flight work and stop the local background process. Saved projects remain available.

Requirements: Node.js 24 or newer, an internet connection, and an Anthropic API account with access to Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5. An installed Edge or Chrome browser enables the optional dynamic-page reader.
