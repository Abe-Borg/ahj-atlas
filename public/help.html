USER GUIDE

From address to evidence.

Local project research for engineers and architects.

UNDERSTAND THE MECHANISMS

Why trust it?

Read what runs, where your data goes, and what the evidence checks can and cannot prove.

Start a project

  1. Open API & spending and enter your Anthropic API key. The connection checks the key; each request checks access to the model it will use. The sidebar shows Claude connected only after Anthropic accepts the key, and Claude disconnected when no key is entered or the key is rejected. Web search must be enabled in your Anthropic Console.
  2. Enter the complete project address and choose your discipline, or select Other — enter your discipline and type your specialty. Add the scope and permit date when known. Building use defaults to Hyperscale data center; choose another listed use, Not yet specified, or Other — enter building use to type your own. For a site without an assigned street address, add a parcel number (APN) or site description under Add project context.
  3. Choose Research now or Research later, and start.
  4. Inspect findings, evidence passages, and questions to resolve. Download a PDF, Excel workbook, or JSON research record.

Real-time and batch

Research now begins immediately. The app updates as each research stage and source reading finishes.

Research later uses Anthropic’s Message Batches API. Model token prices are 50% lower; search fees are unchanged. Research has dependent stages, so it requires several batch submissions. Each batch has a 24-hour processing window and can expire. A complete report can take longer than 24 hours.

Keep the local application running to advance stages automatically. Closing the browser is fine. If the computer sleeps or the backend stops, batches already submitted continue at Anthropic. Reopen the app and reconnect your key to retrieve results and continue.

Fire protection and NFPA standards

Fire protection research screens individual NFPA standards for your project, including system-specific candidates based on building use and notes. Each finding distinguishes applicability from evidence status and gives the adopted edition and source when established. A standard on the checklist is not automatically a legal requirement. Unknown editions remain unconfirmed.

For an older report, open Codes & standards → Research NFPA standards. This starts paid, focused research and a new report. It retains completed jurisdiction/contact work, saved sources, spending and cumulative search/read limits. Choose real-time or batch in the dialog. Your previous report remains available while research runs.

PDF reports include a separate NFPA section, and Excel exports include an NFPA standards worksheet. Add system details such as battery chemistry, suppression type and fuel storage through the normal project-context update when known.

Project chat

A project name is required when creating research. Within a saved project, open Chat with AI to ask about the report, sources, research notes and saved question answers. Choose a Reply depth for each message: Standard (Sonnet 5.5, high reasoning) suits most questions, and Premium (Opus 5.5, high reasoning) is for the hardest analysis and costs about twice as much per token. Each project has its own conversation and draft. Switching projects keeps pending replies with their original project.

Each message starts with the whole saved report, research briefs, source register and excerpts from every retrieved source, plus earlier conversation. The assistant can look up anything else saved for that project and cite sources you can open. It may add general professional knowledge, labeled separately from project evidence. Chat never changes the project on its own.

Actions you approve. Chat can propose five kinds of change: answering a saved question, dismissing one, saving a finding as a note in the report, starting a research round (with new project context, or the focused NFPA standards update on a fire protection project), and correcting the project address or parcel/site description. Each proposal appears below the reply as a card with an Apply button, and nothing changes until you choose it. Apply saves exactly what the card shows, through the same steps as a question card or the Continue research dialog. Answer, dismiss and note cards are free; answers and dismissals can be reopened on the question card. Research and location cards start a paid research round; choose Research now or Research later (batch) on the card. They wait while research, a chat reply or an outstanding request is running, and they can no longer be applied once research has run after they were proposed; ask chat again if the change still applies. An answer chat found in a source names that source, because an applied answer is kept as information you provided. Activity records each applied proposal.

When the saved records don't answer a question, or you ask for current information, the assistant can search the web and read public pages. Pages it reads are added to the project's source register, so later chat, research and the report can use them, and you can open the supporting passage for each cited claim. Search results appear as [Web] Search lead: a snippet to follow up, not verified evidence. The assistant reads only pages it has found in searches, saved sources, links on pages it read, or addresses you include in your message. When the app's own reader cannot open a page (for example, a site that blocks it), the assistant can retrieve it through Anthropic's web fetch instead; those pages join the source register too. Web searches are localized to the project's city and state. Each reply can use up to 20 web searches and 30 page reads (web fetches included), separate from research's allowance.

Report notes. When chat settles a point worth keeping, it can propose a note, or you can ask it to save one. Applying the card is free and adds the note to a Notes section on Overview and to the PDF, Excel and JSON exports. Notes stay when research rebuilds the report. They are labeled as saved from chat and are not re-verified by research. Choose Remove on a note to delete it.

While a reply runs, its text appears as it is written, with the current step, such as the search being run or the source being read. Answers can include headings, lists, tables and code. If Claude declines a message, the reply shows Declined by Claude with the reason and a button to try the same message on the other model, which uses different safeguards.

Each message uses real-time API pricing, and each reply shows its estimated cost. There is no spending limit. A reply can use up to 20 requests, 60 lookups and about 20 minutes of lookups, then writes its final answer from what it found. Web searches cost $10 per 1,000 plus the tokens of their results. Project evidence is cached for an hour, so follow-up questions within the hour cost less. Chat runs alongside research and does not wait for research to finish. Stop reply prevents more work after the current request finishes and its cost is recorded. Conversations are saved locally and sent with relevant project context to Anthropic when you send a message. Restarting the app does not automatically resend interrupted messages.

Adding your own documents

Open the Sources tab and use Add a document you have to add a PDF, HTML or plain-text file up to 50 MB, such as a Fire Marshal's letter, an amendment an AHJ emailed you, or owner or insurer criteria. The app reads its text on this computer and saves it as a source marked your document. Chat can quote it in the next reply; choose Continue research to include it in the research and a new report. Claude treats it as evidence you provided, not as a page retrieved from the issuing agency. Save Word files as PDF first. A scanned PDF needs text recognition (OCR) before it can be added. Adding the same file again updates the same source.

Answering questions

Each Questions to resolve card lets you enter what you know and choose Save answer, or Dismiss a question that is not a priority. The open count updates immediately. Expand Answered & dismissed to edit an answer or reopen a question. Responses are saved with the project and included in exports.

Saving does not start research or use Claude credits. Choose Research with saved answers to start another round with your answers and dismissals. Newly saved information reopens research so its implications can be checked. Answers remain user-provided context; dismissing a question does not establish compliance or non-applicability. All fields under Add project context, including Additional context, are optional.

Understanding evidence

Source-supported: the model identified support and the app matched a quoted passage to retrieved source text. This is not an independent certification of legal applicability or design compliance.

Conditional: the finding depends on an assumption or unresolved jurisdiction. Conflicting: sources disagree. Unconfirmed: the evidence or exact edition has not been established.

Search discoveries are listed separately from sources read as evidence. Scanned PDF pages can be inspected visually, but a visually transcribed quotation remains unconfirmed until readable evidence supports it. Current public contacts, effective dates, transition rules, and adopted editions should be confirmed with the responsible authority when unclear.

Costs and limits

The app uses Sonnet 5.5 for research and Opus 5.5 for an evidence check and the final report. Contacts use medium effort; jurisdiction and code research use high effort; both Opus stages use high effort. The evidence check can investigate missing or conflicting sources before the report is written. Model rates are based on Anthropic’s documentation checked September 28, 2026.

The app does not limit spending. Each project shows its estimated cost to date, the part that came from chat, and an estimate for requests still pending. API & spending shows estimated spending today and across all projects. Estimates use recorded token usage at list prices; native search has variable internal token usage, and the provider invoice is authoritative. To set a hard cap, use your workspace spend limit in Anthropic Console; the app reports when Anthropic stops a request at that limit.

Research is bounded to 80 searches, 100 source reads (including pages fetched through Anthropic's web fetch), 12 requests per stage, and at most two active local research tasks. Each research request can read up to 300,000 tokens of input; a long investigation restarts from its saved checkpoint at 200,000. The final report's reviewer receives up to 600,000 characters of source excerpts and up to 80,000 characters of each research stage's brief. Research responses allow up to 60,000 output tokens; the final report allows 100,000, including thinking. Project chat sets no token limit of its own: each chat request can write up to 128,000 output tokens, the model’s maximum, and read as much input as the model’s context window holds. These are ceilings, not targets or fixed charges. The Opus evidence check has its own limit of 6 requests, 15 searches and 20 source reads within the project totals. Real-time work has an active research time limit of 45 minutes; a response already in flight can finish afterward, with a 30-minute response deadline. Batch waiting time has separate provider deadlines.

If research pauses with unfinished stages, choose Continue research or Finish with saved evidence. A partial report still requires a paid final review. Research stages keep their selected mode unless you explicitly change it on continuation. Chat always uses real-time pricing, including on a batch project.

Stopping, resuming, and uncertain requests

Stop research prevents new requests and asks Anthropic to cancel pending batches. In-flight work can still complete and be billed. The app keeps polling known batches to reconcile results.

If a connection ends during submission, the app keeps the request's estimated cost pending instead of submitting a duplicate. For a batch, find its ID in your Anthropic Console and use Reconcile original batch after it finishes. The app matches the exact request identifier before importing. After the 29-day batch-result retention window, or if results cannot be accessed, the app stops automatic retrieval and keeps the estimate pending for manual reconciliation.

When automatic reconciliation is unavailable, open Activity → Resolve uncertain charge. Confirm in the Anthropic Console that the original request finished, failed, or was canceled, and record its charge. Do not release a reservation for work whose outcome is still unknown.

Adding project context or a follow-up question when resuming reopens the research stages so the findings can be checked. When all research stages are already complete, continuing without new context runs only a paid final report using the saved sources. Older saved reports are preserved; explicitly continuing an older project adds the new Opus evidence check when needed. Recorded costs are retained.

Correcting the project address

You can correct a project's address without deleting the project, for example to fix a typo or ZIP code, or when a new-tract site receives its assigned street address. Choose Continue research (or Review or extend report) and edit Project address or Parcel number (APN) or site description in the dialog. The address needs 8–500 characters, the same as a new project.

A changed address or site description reopens jurisdiction research and the stages that depend on it: contacts, codes, the Opus evidence check and the final report. Activity records the old and new values. The address lookup, evidence excerpts, project chat and PDF, Excel and JSON exports use the corrected address. Exports also list the site description and earlier addresses. Saved sources, recorded costs and search/read limits are kept. Resubmitting the same address does not reopen research.

Wait for running research, an active chat reply or an outstanding request to finish, and resolve any uncertain charge, before correcting the address. An address correction cannot be combined with Finish with saved evidence or the focused NFPA update. Project chat can also propose a correction as a card; applying it follows the same rules, and removing a site description still takes the dialog.

Diagnostics and troubleshooting

Open API & spending → Diagnostics to inspect saved progress, request history, timing, usage, and errors. Choose a project or the entire workspace, then select Download diagnostics to save a troubleshooting file.

Diagnostics are local and persist after restarting. The latest 5,000 detailed events are retained. Older projects still show saved request summaries, but detailed tracing starts with version 1.2.0. Viewing or downloading diagnostics does not spend Claude credits.

Keys, prompts, source text, and model thinking are excluded. Review error details before sharing the file; nothing is uploaded automatically.

Your data and API key

Research is stored in the app’s data folder. If you put the app in a synced folder, that folder’s normal sync settings apply. The local server accepts connections only from this computer.

Your key is held in backend memory by default. Optional Windows storage protects it with your Windows account and saves the encrypted credential in Local AppData, outside the app’s project folder. The key field is not intentionally copied into browser storage, reports, or the research database. Secrets typed into project notes or chat are not excluded by that separation.

Project details and relevant source content are sent to Anthropic to perform research. The address can also be sent to the US Census geocoder when applicable. Public research URLs are retrieved from their original sites. Batch requests are stored by Anthropic during processing; consult its current data-retention documentation for details.

The isolated research browser uses a fresh profile without your browser sign-ins. It reads public pages and does not log into services, submit applications, send emails, or purchase documents.

Launch and stop

Double-click Start AHJ Atlas.cmd to open the application. Double-click Stop AHJ Atlas.cmd to finish in-flight work and stop the local background process. Saved projects remain available.

Requirements: Node.js 24 or newer, an internet connection, and an Anthropic API account with access to Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5. An installed Edge or Chrome browser enables the optional dynamic-page reader.
